## Why this exists

remanence-lib is published under the GNU General Public License v3.0 only.
The owner **reserves the right to relicense the project** on any terms at
any time. Nothing is planned; the reservation exists so the option is not
lost by default.

Relicensing is only possible for a party who holds rights in the whole
work. If parts of remanence-lib were owned by different people, the project
would be permanently fixed under its current licence — not by anyone's
decision, but by arithmetic. This agreement is how the project stays able
to make that choice deliberately rather than losing it by accident.

It has a second purpose that matters just as much. remanence-lib is
copyleft because it is meant to stay free, and a copyleft licence is only
as good as someone's ability to enforce it. **Only a copyright owner can
bring an infringement action.** Consolidated ownership is what makes the
GPL on this project enforceable rather than decorative.

Two commitments run the other way, and they are binding parts of this
agreement, not reassurances:

- **What has been published stays published.** Section 4 puts this in
  operative terms. No relicensing can withdraw a release already made
  under the GPL.
- **You keep the right to your own work.** Section 3 licenses your
  contribution back to you, without restriction. Assigning it here does
  not cost you the use of what you wrote.

## What needs signing, and what does not

This agreement is required for **copyrightable contributions**: source
code, documentation, format definitions, scripts, test fixtures of any
substance, and artwork.

It is **not** required to file a bug report, request a feature, review a
pull request, take part in a discussion, or submit a change too trivial
to attract copyright (a typo fix, a one-line correction). If you are
unsure whether something crosses that line, ask — the answer is usually
"no", and nobody is trying to collect signatures for their own sake.

living persons. German law is the common example: under §29 UrhG,
copyright itself is not assignable, and only exploitation rights may be
granted.

unenforceable, or not permitted under the law applying to you, it takes
effect instead as a grant to the Owner of an **exclusive, irrevocable,

signature alone grants nothing, and the Contribution cannot be accepted

## How to submit a signed agreement

Send the completed document to <paul@galbraiths.ca> before, or alongside,
your first pull request. The Project keeps a durable record linking each
accepted Contribution to its executed agreement.

A statement in a pull request, a commit trailer, or an issue comment is
**not** a substitute for this document, and a Contribution will not be
merged on one.
